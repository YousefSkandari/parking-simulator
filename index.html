Methodology & Assumptions

Agent-Based Model

The simulator uses discrete-event agent-based modelling with 1-minute time resolution. Three agent types interact: Drivers (arriving based on footfall curves, making parking decisions based on utility scoring), Civil Enforcement Officers (patrolling DYL segments, issuing PCNs with realistic timing), and Businesses (tracking revenue from nearby footfall).

Driver Decision Model

Each driver scores available parking options using: utility = f(walking_distance, cost, PCN_risk, convenience). Three archetypes: Quick-stop (40%, 10-30min, £15 avg spend), Destination shopper (35%, 1-2hrs, £48 avg), Commuter (25%, 4-8hrs, £12 avg). Distribution based on TfL travel survey data for West London town centres.

Economic Model

Revenue chain: Policy → Driver parking choices → Footfall at businesses → Revenue per business = baseline × footfall_ratio × dwell_time_factor. Local economic multiplier of 1.4x per NEF methodology. PCN effective revenue accounts for 55% early payment rate, 12% appeal rate (35% success), and 8% write-off.

Limitations

  • Does not model traffic flow, congestion, or road safety impacts
  • Walking distance uses Haversine × 1.3 (not actual pedestrian routing)
  • Business revenues are estimates from ONS benchmarks, not actual accounts
  • No long-term behavioral adaptation modelled beyond seasonal adjustment
  • Single-area model — does not capture displacement to neighbouring centres
